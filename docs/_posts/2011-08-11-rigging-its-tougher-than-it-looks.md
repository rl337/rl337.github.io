So I spent the last few hours trying to figure out why my aperture lines up correctly in one pose
but not in another.  I’m sure that there’s a simple solution to the problem… and looking back I’ll
think, “D’oh! yeah, that was a dumb newb mistake.”   In a previous life, I was told that experiences
like these are “learning opportunities.” sigh.

For anyone curious what a vertically slatted optical assembly looks like
[rigged](http://wiki.blender.org/index.php/Doc:Tutorials/Animation/Armatures/BSoD/Preface:_Rigging_in_Blender)
in [blender](http://blender.org)? Check it out… It’ll change over the next few days I’m
sure, as I figure out my alignment issue, but to date, this is the most complex rig I’ve ever done.
Go me!

[![eyerig-screenshot](/assets/images/legacy-blog/2011/08/eyerig-screenshot.png.webp)](/assets/images/legacy-blog/2011/08/eyerig-screenshot.png.webp)

So
what you’re looking at here are the rig “bones” for the aperture which allow the aperture to open
and close.  Behind it, you see a fan-shaped set of
[bones](http://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Blender_3D:_Noob_to_Pro/Bones) which control the segmented
slats above and below the eye that telescope in or out depending on the inclination of the optical
assembly itself.

.The specific problem that I’m having at the moment is, if I incline the eye, the aperture drifts
slightly to the left.  I’ll experiment on it this weekend and hopefully find a good solution.
Learning Opportunity, right?
